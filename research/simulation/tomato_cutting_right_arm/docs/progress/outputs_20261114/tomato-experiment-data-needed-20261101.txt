Tomato experiment: evidence needed to close material validation
1 November 2026

Current position
The published tomato force curve is a provisional reference. Its extraction is not a new experiment. The simulation still uses synthetic bulk, contact and interface coefficients. No author has been contacted, and the request below has not been sent.

Preferred evidence
Numeric force/displacement or force/time recordings and the matching blade trajectory; specimen geometry or scan; blade profile, pose and cut plane; tissue/ripeness description; force-axis definition, filtering and sensor calibration; loading speed; and separate repeated trials reserved for validation.

A figure alone cannot identify the model uniquely: bulk stiffness, fracture energy, blade geometry and contact response can trade off. Fitting alternating plotted points from one curve is interpolation, not independent validation. Parameters should be frozen before evaluating a separate trial.

Unsent request draft
Subject: Data and modulus-unit clarification for the tomato cutting experiment in your 2019 IROS paper

Dear Dr. Jamdagni and Professor Jia,

We are developing a tomato-cutting simulation and would like to compare it with the tomato experiment in "Robotic Cutting of Solids Based on Fracture Mechanics and FEM."

Would you be willing to share the numerical force/torque and blade-motion histories for the tomato trial, the corresponding specimen scan or dimensions and cutting plane, and any separate repeated trials suitable for validation? Blade geometry, sensor-axis conventions and processing details would also help us reproduce the comparison.

Could you also clarify the intended unit of Young's modulus in Table I? The printed N/m header differs from the pressure unit normally used for a three-dimensional modulus.

We would cite your work and preserve the distinction between calibration and independent validation. Please let us know any conditions for using the data.

Thank you.

If collecting local data instead
Record specimen and blade dimensions, loading trajectory, force axes and calibration together. Save the unfiltered recordings and processing steps. Use separate specimens for fitting and checking the fitted model. Include slow indentation as well as fracture tests so contact/bulk compliance can be distinguished from separation energy. A preliminary protocol should be reviewed against the actual sensor resolution and available apparatus before assigning acceptance tolerances.
