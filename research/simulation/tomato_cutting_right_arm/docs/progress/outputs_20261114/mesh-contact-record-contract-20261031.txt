Mesh contact-record contract — 31 October 2026

Implemented and checked a separate float64 reference contract for particle, edge and face records. Eleven focused tests passed. No native engine, physics step, solver writer or force-producing route was launched.

Installed solver conventions:
- Three soft-corner indices, padded with -1; barycentric weights are nonnegative, sum to one, and use zero padding.
- The rigid witness is the raw, uninflated surface point in BODY-local coordinates, with shape-to-body transformation already applied.
- The normal is rigid-outward in world coordinates. Reference tests use an explicit rigid-facet normal; they do not claim to resolve normals at sharp edges or intersections.
- Soft point = weighted current particle positions. Projected gap = n dot (soft point minus world rigid point). Reference penetration = minus projected gap plus maximum participating particle radius plus shape margin. Radius/margin are counted exactly once.
- Raw surface velocity is body-local. Actual previous/current native history and temporal sampling remain runtime requirements.

Checks reject wrong frame/origin, inconsistent projections, reversed/nonunit/nonfinite normals, invalid barycentrics or particle padding, stale steps, wrong shape/body mapping, double-applied margins, ambiguous intersection-only witnesses and saturated streams. A coincident zero-gap witness with zero radius/margin does not activate the reference force law. No physical load is inferred from a crossing alone.

Duplicate policy is deliberately limited: exact mathematically equivalent reference events may be identified after canonicalizing support/weights. Nearby contacts and different soft-particle supports are retained; their force Jacobians may differ. Raw overflow is rejected before any reduction. No installed native deduplication, contact counts or solver accumulation were changed.

Reaction-observer concern found in source:
The installed solver builds its per-body soft-contact adjacency only for positive effective inverse mass; static/kinematic bodies are skipped. Our existing reaction observer reads that adjacency. A prescribed blade therefore needs a collector over the complete global contact stream, or another independently validated reaction route. Tests distinguish the full stream from dynamic adjacency, including nondeterministic valid adjacency ordering. This is a source-level risk finding, not runtime proof of a positive-contact failure.
