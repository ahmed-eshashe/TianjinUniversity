IMPLEMENTATION AND RUNTIME DECISION — 8 November 2026

Decision: RETAIN FOR RESTRICTED RESEARCH USE; DO NOT ADOPT THE CURRENT v06 CANDIDATE FOR ROBOT CUTTING OR LEARNING.
Close the present feasibility branch with an unmet runtime requirement and incomplete cutting functionality. Preserve all component passes and failures. This is a decision about this laptop implementation and model combination, not a rejection of FEM or the DiSECt paper.

Evidence driving the decision
- Accepted v05 short bulk method: about272 wall seconds/simulated second, exceeding the prospective120 development limit.
- Corrected intact settling:769/1291 wall seconds/simulated second.
- Exact-CAD microscopic contact probe:1840.5/3345.2 wall seconds/simulated second, with a failed5% force comparison and at most two contacting surface nodes.
- New split mesh: static geometry/mass pass, but minimum nodal mass is about142,000 times smaller than the intact mesh minimum. Live cohesion and cut-face dynamics are absent.
- No complete tomato slice or full-cut runtime measured. No qualified replacement or training backend exists.
Different intervals and physical models must not be treated as matched speedups. Contact averages must not be extrapolated into a predicted full-slice duration.

Architecture to retain
1. Isaac Sim robot, kitchen, natural handle grip, disclosed fixed attachment and motion interface remain the presentation/robot layer.
2. Original research solver/checkpoint6925 remains the preserved reference, with its existing limitations.
3. v06 is an offline numerical/material/contact reference only. Keep its saved states, controls and mass-conserving geometry. Do not automatically port the47-patch law into it or add another integrator.
4. The learning interface may remain engine-independent. No training, success labels or demonstration of separation may be generated from an unqualified cutting engine.

Alternatives assessed from existing evidence
A. Continue building v06: not selected. Requires live cohesive/history/closure implementation, tiny-cell handling, contact coverage/force checks and major acceleration. Existing timings do not justify this investment as a bounded feasibility task.
B. Adopt patched native DiSECt: not selected yet. Its prism runs around1.7wall s/s but differs in mesh, material and contact; two springs remain intact. Native tomato preparation fails. Fast prism results cannot qualify our tomato.
C. Adopt the tested RoboNinja MPM configuration: not selected. Earlier block tests cost18–24wall s/s, use different resolution/material/contact, show board-clearance failure and do not demonstrate separation. GPU/training memory is also unresolved.
D. Build a deliberately simpler learning model: a possible future proposal, not authorized here. It would change the agreed representation/accuracy contract. The user's request to extend the research model only remains in force. No proxy, mesh coarsening, artificial slice release or hidden material tuning is implemented.

Execution directive for Sol or a later continuation
- Do not start section4, rerun intact settling, continue a timestep/material sweep, or expand v06 into a full custom cutting engine under the previous generic continuation request.
- Treat section1 as historical component evidence, section2 as3/4 with speed failed, and section3 as3/5 narrowly scoped with two open gates. Do not erase those passes or mark full readiness.
- Keep experimental validation pending. Keep original production paused and preserve source/evidence.
- The next engineering commitment must specify one representation, one implemented fracture/contact mechanism, one measurable runtime target and one bounded exit criterion. A change to the model or accuracy contract must be presented concretely to the user before implementation.
- If the user elects to keep developing v06 despite the cost, first propose a narrowly bounded saved-event contact-convergence test and offline split-conditioning assessment. Do not automatically launch them. Passing those alone would not reverse this runtime decision.

Runtime budgeting for the next proposal
The old120wall-s/s limit was a development ceiling, not a practical RL throughput target. Derive a learning budget from total rollout count and episode duration: affordable wall-s/s = available wall seconds / (rollout count * simulated episode seconds). For illustration only,1000episodes of5s within24hours require <=17.28wall s/s before resets, policy work and failed episodes. This arithmetic is not a benchmark or a promise. Agree the intended workload before choosing a training engine.

Current outcome
The implementation decision is complete. The project has useful validated components but no adopted fast tomato-cutting backend. Section4 remains gated. Do not substitute endless repair work for a new, explicit feasibility decision.
