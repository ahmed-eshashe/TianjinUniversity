V23 — FIRST PHYSICAL CONTACT INTEGRATION; QUALIFICATION REMAINS OPEN
12 November 2026

Outcome
The v22 bulk runtime now has an isolated, state-driven blade-contact adapter.
It applies the original saved CAD blade and the previously declared v02 normal
potential/friction law to the same affine deformation used by material stress.
The original material, contact/fracture parameters, force and penetration limits
were not changed. The Isaac/DiSECt setup is unchanged; 2,108 protected files match.

This is a 6 mm supported material cube, NOT a tomato. No fracture topology was
enabled, no physical slice was made, and no complete RL environment was tested.
The physical gate remains open because contact sampling has not converged under
the retained 5% force criterion. The finer tests also demonstrate that this dense
contact implementation is currently unsuitable for the intended RL throughput.

What is verified
59 selected operator/geometry/work-adapter checks pass. Actual CAD contact force,
normal potential, resultant, moment and power mapping agree with independent
references. The final 80-step loaded CPU/GPU comparison has maximum position
error 0.211 nanometres, velocity error 2.95 micrometres/s and F error 8.81e-8.
A deliberately excessive force rolls back mechanical state, clock, impulse and
work. A new instance initializes independently. Unqualified cut surfaces are
rejected rather than accepted by the intact-tet contact adapter.

Closed affine material tetrahedra are bounded with signed distance, a covering
radius, adaptive subdivision, motion allowance and a 2 micrometre query allowance.
The bound covers the full declared volume and the piecewise-linear swept path;
it is not a point-center clearance test. Deep penetration is rejected. Accepted
press/hold/release cycles keep the 2 N and 0.1 mm stops. Early edge, near-miss,
broad-face and tangential-slide cycles have finite, positive volumes and proper
work/impulse balance; these are component controls, not fracture qualification.
No board-contact or fragment-recontact qualification is inferred from fixed nodes.

An independent loaded reference exposed contact-coordinate roundoff. The isolated
correction retains the exact original CAD vertices, recomputes closest-triangle
distance and normal in float64 after BVH candidate lookup, and uses a float64 tool
clock. The bulk operator and material constants remain unchanged. Early failed
comparison evidence is preserved. One early ledger test incorrectly expected a
uniform nodal marker to yield despite tiny nodal inertia; the corrected test uses
a defined center-of-mass marker and preserves the same physical law and limits.

Existing work law, not a new cutting claim
The preserved v02 ideal rate-independent cutting resistance uses Gc=80 J/m^2;
this synthetic parameter and law are NOT the old DiSECt cohesive law. Its new
adapter uses the exact reduced inverse inertia, rather than the old particle-mass
shortcut. Stick/slip/reversal/ineligible controls close the kinetic/tool-work,
fracture-work and identified numerical-loss ledger. Area previews do not commit
history; unpaid and duplicate-area cases remain guarded. This is a standalone
work-law component test. It is not coupled blade-driven physical separation.
New cut surfaces, paid topology commits, recontact and loaded reset remain open.

Refinement results
Material: compressible neo-Hookean E=100 kPa, nu=0.4, rho=1000 kg/m^3.
Fixture: 6 mm cube, 48 linear tetrahedra, 27 nodes, h=3 mm. Fixed bottom nodes.
CAD: 74 vertices / 144 triangles, unchanged. One 13 ms shallow contact cycle.
Normal contact K=1e9 N/m^3; friction coefficient 0.2; regularization speed 1 mm/s.
Uniform surface quadrature spacing is an integration resolution, not mesh spacing.

Temporal comparison at 125 micrometre surface spacing, dt=6.25 vs 3.125 us:
- maximum shared-time force discrepancy 1.490%, passes 5%; impulse 0.574%;
- tool work 0.217%; friction work 0.185%; position 0.0445 micrometres;
- material-weighted velocity upper RMS discrepancy 0.155 mm/s, below 5 mm/s.
This temporal pass does not establish spatial convergence or a physical cut.

Spatial comparison at dt=3.125 us, spacing 62.5 vs 31.25 micrometres:
- force discrepancy reaches 59.36% near release at 10.25 ms; limit is 5%;
- force magnitudes there are about 0.1713 and 0.1075 mN. This is a small absolute
  load, but it exceeds the pre-existing 0.1 mN denominator floor and cannot be
  discarded or passed by changing the criterion after seeing the result;
- position differs by 0.1646 micrometres; weighted velocity upper RMS by 0.503 mm/s;
- impulse 0.157%, tool work 0.581%, friction work 0.325% all pass;
- the largest absolute force difference anywhere in the cycle is 0.2405 mN.
Only the force criterion fails in this final spatial comparison. No tolerance
was relaxed. Still finer uniform sampling was not expanded into another long
campaign: its measured cost grows rapidly and does not offer an RL solution.
Material-mesh spatial convergence is a separate, still-uncompleted requirement.

Performance and diagnosis
Parallel force mapping and a staged deterministic force-stop reduction remove
serial accumulation hotspots. An exact CAD bounding-box exclusion avoids distance
queries where the force is provably zero. It does not replace the blade by its
box or convex hull. A convexity audit finds the actual CAD is nonconvex; replacing
it by its convex hull would change the shape and is not adopted.

Matched 62.5 um / 3.125 us contact cycle, same positions/velocities/force histories:
  before bounding-box exclusion: 22.517 wall seconds
  after bounding-box exclusion:  11.084 wall seconds
All compared trajectory/force differences are zero. This is about 2.03x faster.
The final separately measured profile takes 10.535 wall seconds for 0.013 sim s:
  GPU advancement and host observation: 9.142 s
  conservative whole-volume certificate: 0.399 s
  accepted state transfer:               0.0025 s
  remaining observations, metrics, logging and control overhead: about 0.992 s
Initial surface generation/GPU setup are outside this episode timer; initial graph
capture is included. These are diagnostic contact costs, not full RL transitions.
The ratio is about 810 wall seconds per simulated second for this one tiny coupon.
The profile tracks 43.59 MB of device buffers; process peak RSS is 593,892 KiB.
Neither figure includes a robot, kitchen or policy. General batched contact was
not measured. The finest 31.25 um comparison uses 887,808 samples and takes 41.95 s.

The previous v22 103-109 geometric transitions/s result is preserved and remains
valid in its stated scope. It omitted physical contact/fracture/robot/policy and
must not be described as full tomato or RL performance. This test is different in
size, resolution, timestep and environment count; it is not an equal-model speed
comparison with v22 or DiSECt.
