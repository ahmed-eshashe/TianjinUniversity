Documentation clarification
The NVIDIA PhysX5.6 articulation guide specifies that incoming joint forces are averages over a simulation step. It also distinguishes the reported joint-internal mechanisms (drive, explicitly applied torque, friction, velocity clamping and joint limits) from armature/tendon effects. Thus the previous blanket statement that interval averaging was undocumented is too broad: it is documented at SDK level. The installed Python tensor API describes projection/frame conventions but does not independently establish that the captured installed binary reproduces all SDK assumptions. This is a versioned clarification, not a rewrite of historical reports or runtime qualification.

Primary reference: https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.6.0/docs/Articulations.html#link-incoming-joint-force

Required work definition
A meaningful motor-energy input is the integral of actual generalized motor torque times actual joint velocity, with dissipative terms declared separately. An interval-average torque and endpoint velocities do not alone determine this integral: they omit within-step torque/velocity correlation. Multiplying a mean torque by net joint displacement is not automatically an exact discrete solver-work identity. In addition, the reported transmitted active-axis load includes multiple mechanisms, so its motor-only interpretation needs independent justification. Agreement of two projections of the same incoming-wrench data does not supply that justification.

This does NOT mean a complete discrete work ledger is impossible. It means it has not been demonstrated for the installed corrected-controller setup. A justified ledger must specify where motor input, joint/rigid-body dissipation, rotor energy, external pressure exchange, supports, contact remapping and numerical dissipation enter, and verify the relevant measurements independently. Existing strict work/force gates cannot be satisfied by relabelling the measured-load channel or selecting a favorable quadrature.
