Conclusion
There are two immediate contact prerequisites (work/force measurement and bounded final approach), one later material implementation gap (accepted live fracture), and several dependent or intentionally deferred checklist items. They should not all be treated as independent active debugging tasks. No new simulation was run in this audit; no scientific gate was closed or relaxed.

1. Large joint-effort offset — resolved within the documented readout scope
The previous audit identified and reconstructed the omitted descendant feedforward bias. Use rl/tomato_tgs_feedforward_readback.py and work/astra_effort_resolution_20261102/verification01. 2. Motor work and whole-system force/work — immediate unresolved blocker
The 155pJ maximum held-out reconstruction discrepancy compares two estimators sharing the SAME midpoint velocity. It is not a measurement of complete physical energy error, nor a precision bound for the current implicit-drive controller. The old PGS force/moment failures are a different test configuration and remain failed; the TGS correction does not fix them.

New saved-data check: across eight known-input intervals, midpoint-velocity motor work is about-80.48microJ while raw joint-displacement work is about+1.856microJ. This does not select either as truth. It demonstrates that substituting recorded joint differences is not a validated remedy in these tiny-coordinate-update records. The conditional float32 endpoint-velocity storage envelope is only2.95pJ for this estimator; it excludes solver, torque reconstruction, configuration and temporal errors. It is neither a total error bound nor permission to dismiss the155pJ residual as rounding.

There is also an information gap independent of arithmetic: interval-average torque and endpoint velocities/positions do not uniquely determine work. audit.py supplies an exact analytic counterexample with identical mean torque, endpoint velocities and displacement, but different torque–velocity covariance and hence work. This is an information counterexample, not a claim that the synthetic paths occurred in PhysX. A controller-specific discrete identity could remove this ambiguity only if derived from the actual solver and independently checked.

Next action: finish one measurement specification before another simulator launch. Enumerate implicit drive contribution, explicit feedforward, rotor kinetic energy, gravity, body damping, external contact and any active passive constraints. For each, state whether the measurement is interval impulse, endpoint value or within-step work and identify its time/frame convention. Require a justified solver-discrete work identity or direct within-step drive impulse/velocity information, plus a numerical precision budget for the intended signal. Existing post-step load plus endpoint fields alone do not establish this. If the installed interface cannot provide the required information within the existing architecture, report that exact instrumentation limitation and present the architectural decision; do not silently alter controller, physical damping, time-step limits, tolerances or benchmark claims.

Finite exit: one source/interface feasibility assessment producing either an implementable measurement contract with independent validation and an unchanged-gate error budget, or a concrete unsupported-interface finding. No new native run until the former exists. Repeating the same120-step capture cannot answer missing within-step information. Do not relaunch historical PGS/solver/damping cohorts.
