Decision needed: speed for learning versus unchanged detailed simulation
2026-11-13 — isolated v27 candidate; production setup unchanged

Outcome
The requested visible, physically checked, RL-speed tomato has NOT been achieved.
This is a development prototype, not an accepted replacement for Isaac/DiSECt.

Measured progress
- Original CAD contact search retains the exact triangle geometry and contact law.
  80 exhaustive-versus-hierarchical search cases pass on intact and cracked states.
- Whole-material clearance: 120 boundary/sliver/deep-intersection controls pass.
  Device-resident checks additionally pass 32 coverage cases and two rollback tests.
- Intact full-shape tomato: 13 ms contact trajectory reduced from 4.91 s to
  1.30 s in the host-checked loop; the device-resident single-instance pilot
  takes 0.415 s. These are limited contact tests, not cuts or learning episodes.
- Eight independent device-resident tomato runs: 8 x 13 ms in 3.098 s.
  Equivalent 50 Hz material-loop budget: 1.679 transitions/s versus the
  project minimum of 100 COMPLETE environment transitions/s. This is NOT a
  measured RL rate. It excludes fracture transactions, robot, policy, reward,
  reset and rendering, and is not a bound on all possible GPU implementations.
- A matched 3 ms shallow-crack segment takes 4.681 s instead of 33.583 s.
  Force, field and work comparisons pass in this limited segment.
- A longer run stops at global time 9.525 ms because clearance cannot be
  certified within the unchanged 0.1 mm limit. This is an unresolved bound,
  not proof of a physical penetration violation. No full slice is qualified.

Correctness issue found and corrected
Merging affine coefficients with matching material history could remove an
existing crack-opening mode. In the 1 ms update trial the opening rank became
zero transiently, although force and energy differences were small. That trial
is rejected. The new merge rule preserves the COMPLETE previous admissible
state space, and the mechanics/short continuation checks pass. Coefficient
count grows when this is physically necessary; constant memory cannot be
obtained by closing cracks. More cuts and fragment recontact remain untested.

Why this is a scope decision
The user requested unchanged physics and the existing <=5% response agreement.
Local speed improvements do not demonstrate that this detailed, changing-
geometry representation can reach the RL target. Further research on another
numerical representation is possible, but a completion time or speed pass is
not established by these measurements. No successful-cut or RL claim should
be inferred from playback, a visible groove, a geometry-only benchmark, or
matched force on a short intact trajectory.

Choice A — separate approximate training model (recommended if RL speed is
now the priority)
Keep the current Isaac/DiSECt setup and detailed reference completely intact.
Authorize a separate, explicitly approximate model for policy training. This
is a change to the training physics/accuracy contract, not another equivalent
code optimization. The initial candidate would use a compact deformable
representation with persistent cut connectivity and GPU-resident contact;
the specific representation must pass a bounded pilot before selection.
No official CRESSim or BladeMaster capability is assumed or inherited.

Concrete acceptance and stop conditions for that separate attempt:
1. Freeze its effective material/contact/cutting laws, approximation choices,
   operating envelope and reference comparisons BEFORE speed measurements.
2. First demonstrate two independent pieces after actual blade interaction,
   withdrawal without rebonding, an intact bridge during a partial cut, and a
   second intersecting cut. Geometry-only tests do not count as physical cuts.
3. Preserve mass/history, finite-state and capacity stops; keep existing robot
   force and clearance stops for later robot integration. No animated swaps,
   hidden piece deletion or claimed experimental calibration.
4. Measure force, position, work and energy differences against the detailed
   reference. The existing 5% agreement would no longer automatically be a
   hard entry gate for the APPROXIMATE training prototype. All discrepancies
   must be displayed and its policy-use envelope agreed before deployment.
5. Only after the mechanics pilot: original tomato exterior, multiple sizes,
   online blade actions, full slice/withdrawal, further cuts, reset and a
   complete batched environment benchmark including observations/rewards.
6. Require >=100 complete transitions/s at 50 Hz control on this laptop with
   GPU memory headroom. Render a live Isaac demonstration only after passing;
   rendering FPS and recorded playback do not count as physics speed.
7. Limit the first mechanics/speed feasibility pilot to two focused hours;
   record a clear pass/fail and stop this route if it cannot preserve the
   tested fragments or lacks credible throughput headroom. No guaranteed
   completion time or performance claim is made in advance.

Choice B — continue the unchanged detailed-physics research route
Keep the existing response criteria as hard gates. The next research task is
an isolated GPU-resident topology/constraint redesign plus resolving the
clearance qualification, rather than more superficial contact optimizations.
A full physically qualified cut comes before any RL claim. Fast, interactive
training and its completion date remain unproven. This choice preserves the
current accuracy contract but does not satisfy the user's near-term speed
objective on the evidence obtained so far.

No scope change has been executed. Experimental validation remains pending.
