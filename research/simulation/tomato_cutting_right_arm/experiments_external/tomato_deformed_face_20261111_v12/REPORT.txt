Deformed-material and finer-front study — final bounded result, 11 November 2026

RESULT
The isolated GPU candidate now creates and extends a geometric partial cut in a
freely deforming, initially nonuniformly compressed/bent block. Fine-front state
transfer, mass, restart and sufficiently fine mechanical timestep checks pass.
The combined blade-update/refinement accuracy check FAILS. Do not proceed to
physical knife contact, claim a qualified tomato cut, or promote this to RL.
The current Isaac/DiSECt setup remains unchanged. No dependencies were installed.

This study used one baseline (v11) and one diagnosed correction (v12), within the
same two-hour scope. All v10 and earlier evidence, including the failed v11
results, is preserved. The checks finished in well under the campaign budget;
there is no background simulation still running.

WHAT CHANGED
1. Front-aligned quadratic knot insertion replaces the 3 mm whole-support delay.
   A static 50-micrometre (0.05 mm) score can now enable an opening only behind
   its visited front, while the uncut bridge remains connected. The old complete
   displacement and velocity fields are prolonged exactly into the new space.
   Full mass-matrix pullback, virtual-work pullback, strain and energy checks
   accompany insertion. Extra modes deliberately change discrete inertia and
   stiffness spectra; equal transferred state does not mean identical dynamics.
   Refinement inserts tensor layers across the cross-section. It is not a fully
   local 3D adaptive mesh and is not yet a scalable arbitrary-cut representation.
2. v11's exact planar centreline map passed selected non-affine analytic fields
   but rejected an actual freely evolved field. The rejected surface varied by
   less than a micrometre in the short probe; exact planarity was too restrictive.
   v12 instead certifies a conservative reference patch inside the real swept
   finite-width CAD cutting face (original triangles 15/14/16 and 16/17/15).
   An inscribed rectangle uses the actual minimum half-width 0.4078507 mm.
   No hypothetical blade thickening, material rescaling or contact-law change
   was used. The selected planar patch approximates the centreline preimage;
   the full material may deform nonuniformly and its exact preimage may curve.
3. Continuous spline coefficient bounds constrain the normal offset to 10 um
   and curved-front spread to 10 um in this candidate. Monotonicity and common
   finite edge coverage give an inner visited patch, rather than a bounding-box
   fill. A cumulative monotone proof chain can join visited curved-front strips;
   withdrawal/lateral repositioning resets that chain. Negative disjoint-stroke
   tests preserve their skipped material band. All cut history remains material-
   attached and no future trajectory or cut plane is supplied to the selector.

SPATIAL AND TEMPORAL QUALIFICATION
The earlier 3.90625e-7 s mechanical timestep was insufficient for fine-front
velocities. Sparse original observation points missed the problem. At actual
near-front points, halving that timestep changed velocity by 0.3564 m/s, despite
sub-micrometre position and well-converged material-work results. That v11
failure was retained; its criteria were not loosened.

A read-only tangent-mode audit explains why small spatial changes are costly:
the intact block's fastest linearized period was about 171 us; a 50 um front
introduced a 3.83 us period, and an extra 25 um layer a 1.55 us period. These are
local linearization estimates, not nonlinear stability guarantees. The original
timestep provided only about 10 or 4 steps per new fast period respectively.

v12 tested dt=2.44140625e-8 s (16 times finer) against half that timestep, without
damping, mass lumping, altered stiffness or changed physical stops. For the same
loaded 50 um partial-cut state over 0.1 ms:
  whole-field displacement difference bound: 8.79e-10 m
  whole-field velocity difference bound:     0.002716 m/s (screen 0.005 m/s)
  relative applied material-work difference: 2.54e-8
  primary energy residual:                  -4.02e-12 J
  primary/half GPU rollout wall time:        2.95 / 5.20 s
These opening loads are material test forces, not knife reactions or fracture work.

COMBINED FREELY DEFORMING GPU TEST
The same nonuniformly prestrained block evolved freely while the commanded CAD
edge advanced in two 50 us actions. The reference patch was selected online.
After the first action its conservative depth was 44.15 um; after the second,
82.24 um. The commanded geometric advance is not the actual depth in deforming
material. The block remains one connected body with an intact bridge.

Mass remained 0.001728 kg. Cut insertion preserved existing state, and a loaded
restart reproduced the continuation exactly. Independent dense crossing checks
confirmed that sampled points of each new patch had been traversed by the actual
edge face under the declared endpoint interpolation. The continuous coverage
argument uses coefficient bounds; dense samples are an independent check, not
the sole geometric certificate.

Maximum bounds in the primary combined run:
  normal offset from edge centre plane: 0.767 um
  curved-front spread:                  3.493 um
  mechanical half-step position:        1.40e-10 m
  mechanical half-step velocity:        0.001099 m/s
  energy residual over 0.1 ms:          -1.83e-12 J

THE REMAINING FAILED GATE
Repeating the same blade route with 25 us action intervals at the finer mechanical
timestep changes local front refinement as well as activation times. Results:
  whole-field displacement difference bound: 0.943 um (position screen passes)
  whole-field velocity difference bound:     0.3682 m/s (0.005 m/s screen FAILS)
  actual dense near-front velocity witness:  0.3662 m/s at 2050 sampled points
The failure is therefore not merely a loose coefficient bound. Topology remains
partial in both runs and energy/mass checks pass; those do not override the local
velocity failure. Timing and approximation-space changes are confounded in this
comparison. It does not prove that timing alone or spatial resolution alone is
responsible. No further correction was attempted in this bounded study.

COST AND SCOPE
Primary combined rollout: 2.856 s for 0.0001 simulated seconds, about 28,563 wall
seconds per simulated second. This is a single tiny-block diagnostic, not an
episode-time forecast or an RL benchmark. It is an expensive accuracy reference,
not an efficient production solver recommendation. The smaller timestep solves
one accuracy issue but worsens the already open throughput problem.

The model is still the synthetic 12 mm block: E=100 kPa, nu=0.4, rho=1000 kg/m^3,
full consistent mass, order-5 integration, 512 original observation points and
additional front modes. No tomato exterior, gravity, support board, robot, knife
reaction, friction, recontact or irreversible material-failure law was included.
Gc remains unused. The 2 N and 0.1 mm physical contact limits were retained but
not exercised or passed by these geometry-only tests. Endpoint interpolation is
not a proof of continuous physical knife contact or full-material nonpenetration.
General curved cut surfaces, arbitrary orientations and long multi-cut episodes
remain unsupported. Experimental validation remains pending.
