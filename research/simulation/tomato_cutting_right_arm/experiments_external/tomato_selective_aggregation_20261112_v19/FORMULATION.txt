Research basis and limits
Coombs, Bird and Pretti (2025), The Aggregated Material Point Method, DOI 10.1016/j.cma.2025.118012, discusses support-dependent selection as a possible alternative to aggregating every boundary node, with cautions about material distribution and threshold dependence. The present selector and cutting adaptation are our own experiment, not their validated parameter recommendation. The coupled reduced mass must not be relumped.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2025.118012
Prenter et al., Stability and Conditioning of Immersed Finite Element Methods: Analysis and Remedies, describes selective aggregation to limit changes in effective boundary resolution. This provides motivation, not proof of arbitrary-cut dynamic accuracy or RL speed.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11831-023-09913-0

Preserved limitations: simple block geometry; no work-driven fracture, physical blade contact or fragment recontact; incomplete root locality around general partial crack fronts; small fragments without roots retain potentially restrictive time scales; no inherited spatial convergence or experimental calibration. Full arbitrary-path cutting, shape/size behavior, robot integration and environment throughput require their own later qualification.

APPROVED VELOCITY-NORM AMENDMENT, 12 NOVEMBER 2026
The user explicitly selected "Use the material-weighted velocity check (Recommended)" after reviewing VELOCITY_CRITERION_PROPOSAL.txt. This applies to cross-representation, projection and timestep comparisons; strict CPU/GPU same-operator tolerances remain unchanged.
For each material polyhedron p, let d_p be the maximum Euclidean velocity difference across its vertices. The accepted velocity error is sqrt(sum_p rho V_p d_p^2 / sum_p rho V_p), bounded by 5 mm/s. Since the velocity difference is affine on each convex polyhedron, this is a conservative upper bound on the mass-weighted continuum RMS norm. It is not a vector average and cannot hide opposing signs through cancellation. Maximum local velocity remains reported without being the acceptance norm. The norm is evaluated at recorded comparison times, not a proof of continuous-time bounds. Position remains the maximum over all material vertices at those times.
The approved rerun selected support fraction 1e-8 and 12.5 microsecond material substeps across the eight tested 24 mm blocks. Do not generalize this timestep to an arbitrary new cut history, tiny fragment, material, load or resolution. The earlier 1e-16 selector and rejected 25 microsecond runs are preserved.

GPU NUMERICS AND ONE EQUIVALENT OPTIMIZATION
The float32 implementation reduces per-region shape gradients through A, evaluates the same neo-Hookean energy/stress, and applies the inverse of each exact reduced mass block (formed/factored in CPU float64, uploaded as float32). No mass relumping or new diagonal inertia is used. Difference-form strain gradients reproduce translation without summing an absolute-position field.
Staggered velocity Verlet stores v_(n-1/2). Initialization subtracts half an acceleration step from physical velocity; export adds half a current acceleration step. External-action changes reconstruct physical velocity before reinitializing the half-step state. Loaded restart follows the same conversion.
The initial two-kernel formulation repeated nodal force gathering for each nonzero inverse-mass entry. One diagnosed optimization caches each nodal force once before applying coupled mass blocks; this uses three kernels for mass blocks larger than four and retains two for smaller blocks. Matched fused/cached tests found identical exported fields in the tested 60 mm block at batches one/eight. CUDA graphs group 64 material substeps. Each public step backs up state and checks the invalid-volume/nonfinite flag before acceptance, rolling back rejected state. Energy is an independently evaluated test screen, not a runtime energy-correction mechanism. Physical blade-force and penetration enforcement do not exist in this bulk-only candidate and remain unqualified.
