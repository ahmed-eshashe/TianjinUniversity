Recommended optional change
For cross-representation and timestep comparisons of the deformable material, replace the 5 mm/s maximum-pointwise velocity acceptance test with a 5 mm/s mass-weighted RMS velocity bound. Keep the maximum-pointwise velocity error in every report as a diagnostic. Retain all other existing limits, including maximum material-position error 0.1 mm; force/impulse/work error 5%; numerical/projection energy error 5%; mass and momentum checks; positive admissible material volume; 2 N blade force stop; whole-material blade penetration limit 0.1 mm. CPU/GPU same-operator implementation checks retain their separate strict precision tolerances.

Conservative definition used in the diagnostic
For each retained material polyhedron p, compute d_p as the largest velocity-difference norm at its vertices. Both numerical fields are affine within the same polyhedron, so d_p bounds the difference throughout that polyhedron. With actual material mass m_p=rho*V_p, use sqrt(sum(m_p*d_p^2)/sum(m_p)). This bounds the mass-weighted continuum L2 velocity error from above. It is not a centroid-only sample, unweighted mean or a cancellation of opposite errors. Every material region remains represented; no tiny material is deleted. Correspondence and mass must be established explicitly in any comparison with a different topology.

Consequence and limitation
This permits differences in rapid local motion of extremely small material regions. It does not permit large position errors, missed blade intersections, contact-force/work violations, or relabeling numerical loss as fracture. It is a deliberate change in the validation contract, not evidence that the old velocity test passed. The maximum-local-velocity requirement is retained unless the user chooses this option. A task-relevant error norm is reasonable to investigate for RL, but the later blade-contact tests must establish whether the permitted local differences affect cutting. The paper motivating aggregation does not certify this particular metric, selector or tomato model.
