FIRST-PHASE EXECUTION RESULT — 10 November 2026

Decision: Gates 0, 1 and 2 pass within their declared COMPONENT scope.
The early decision phase is complete, well inside its eight-hour cap.
Continue considering this independent TLMPM backend as a research candidate.
Do not adopt it as the tomato engine or call it ready for interactive RL.
No Isaac/DiSECt simulation, robot, kitchen, grip or controller was changed.

WHAT WAS BUILT
A new, isolated, GPU total-Lagrangian MPM prototype with an independent NumPy
reference. It uses installed Warp 1.17.0 and a task-local compilation cache.
No packages or system drivers were installed. The previous public CRESSim
source, build, results and the DiSECt evidence remain preserved. A separate
copy of CRESSim was used for one numerical diagnostic; it is not the selected
backend. The implementation follows the persistent ternary-code/compatible
transfer idea in BladeMaster, with explicitly different constitutive and
integration choices. It is not official BladeMaster code or a reproduction.

GATE 0 — FREEZE AND CONTROL SENSITIVITY: PASS
manifest.json records versions, dependencies, processes and 369 protected-file
hashes. contract.json and architecture.md freeze scope, formulas, units,
thresholds and stops. asset_inventory.json additionally records the existing
robot launcher, arm scene/plan, controller and tomato assets. No old long run
was restarted.
The prior CRESSim absent-cutter coupling failure was reproduced at 0 and 17
degrees, with translated grid placement, while held-tool and wide-gap
controls isolated the pieces. A one-line correction in a separate source copy
proved the grid-mass cutoff was a major cause of lost free translation:
corrected wide-gap translation retains 99.99987% of the applied velocity.
However, the absent-cutter control still transfers approximately 50.06% of
that velocity to the other half. That diagnosis does not repair fracture.
A separate APIC scaling concern is documented, not silently patched/adopted.

GATE 1 — INTACT MECHANICS: PASS AT REFINED SETTINGS
The provisional material is compressible neo-Hookean, E=100 kPa, nu=0.4,
rho=1000 kg/m3, with a defined energy and its analytic stress derivative.
It is neither calibrated tomato tissue nor the prior corotational material.
Initial 6/3 mm grid/particle spacing failed angular momentum and dynamic
half-step velocity screens. Temporal refinement alone did not fix angular
drift. One preplanned numerical correction adopted 3/1.5 mm spacing and
12.5 microsecond steps (6.25 microsecond refinement). Physical size, density,
material and acceptance limits were not relaxed. The old failure is retained.

At this setting, the 60 mm block contains 64,000 particles and weighs 0.216 kg.
Checks passed: basis completeness; energy gradient; frame objectivity;
CPU/GPU stress and short dynamics; unloaded rest and translation; rotated
rest; free spin; affine compression/unload; dynamic compression release;
mass/momentum; positive admissible volume; and half-timestep comparison.
Free-spin angular drift: 0.354% (1% screen).
Compression half-step maximum position difference: 0.00124 mm (0.1 mm screen).
Maximum velocity difference: 3.31 mm/s (5 mm/s screen).
See intact_controls.json and reports/gate1_coarse_fail.json.

GATE 2 — PERSISTENT TOPOLOGY: PASS WITH EXPLICIT LIMITS
Particles keep fixed reference coordinates and persistent ternary cut history.
Finite, current tool sweeps assign labels; no future cutting trajectory is
passed to the backend. Compatible nodal momentum exchanges conserve mass and
momentum; opposing codes do not reconnect when the tool disappears. Codes
are assigned without particle duplication or rebuilding the remainder.

Verified outcomes:
- Untouched block remains one connected body.
- Partial score retains one connected body and transmits a probe through its
  intact bridge. It is not prematurely divided into two global bodies.
- Full sweep produces two mechanically independent components after withdrawal.
- Two parallel sweeps produce three components; the middle slice is independent.
- Two intersecting sweeps produce four independent components.
- Four sweeps produce nine components, with 25 active logical codes.
- Halving the timestep preserves full/partial cut labels and bridge verdicts.
- Translated grid placement and rotated tool/material fixtures retain the split.
- A single fragment dynamically rotates about 1.12 degrees relative to its
  neighbor while translating; history remains unchanged and independence holds.
- A short finite blade edge leaves surrounding material connected; a near miss
  does not assign cuts. A fifth cut is rejected without changing the state.
- Actual occupied-grid mass agrees with particle mass across all cut counts.

The post-sweep gaps were opened by declared external test velocities and
forward dynamics. No empty gap was substituted as the initial cutting state.
Paired unforced/probed continuations had no tool, gravity, supports or contact
barrier. The single-cut surface-gap estimate was approximately 4 mm. The
untouched half's mean probe response was 0.0000039% of the imposed velocity,
well below the 1% limit. Middle-slice and rotating-fragment supplemental
checks also passed stricter individual-particle leakage screens.
See persistent_cut_controls.json, gate2_supplement.json and raw snapshots.

These are GEOMETRY-DRIVEN CUTTING/TRANSFER FIXTURES. They do not establish
force-triggered fracture, cutting resistance, skin puncture, strict blade
nonpenetration, fragment recontact, a CAD-knife cut or a finished tomato.
Tool-driven labels are not an experimentally validated failure mechanism.
Within-step blade rotation/curved paths and cutting already-moving pieces
remain unqualified. The tested straight-segment envelope must not be expanded
by interpretation. An intact bridge test is a mechanics result, not a visual
red texture or a scripted fragment animation.

MEASURED COST — THE IMPORTANT REMAINING LIMITATION
Synchronized, warmed physics-only windows of 0.02 simulated seconds:
  cuts       active codes       wall seconds / simulated second
    0               1                       61.2
    1               3                       69.9
    2               9                       71.1
    4              25                       88.3
These are preliminary short windows, not repeated confidence intervals.
They exclude setup, logging, rendering, new contact, robot coupling and RL.
They do not establish equal physical accuracy to CRESSim or DiSECt. Compared
with the old 8000-particle CRESSim probe, this candidate has eight times as many
particles and eight times as many substeps, plus different mechanics/history.
At 50 Hz, this single-environment cost is only about 0.57–0.82 transitions/s
before adding missing components, far below the 100 transitions/s entry target.
No real-time or efficient online-RL claim is justified.

Allocated numerical arrays: approximately 108.3 MiB per instance, reserving
81 code slots while computing on active slots. Campaign sampled GPU peak:
1824 MiB with several test instances retained simultaneously, including CUDA
runtime/allocator overhead; this is not single-environment GPU usage.
Campaign host peak: approximately 1208 MiB. Resource sampling began partway
through the campaign. More efficient memory layout and complete-loop cost
remain future measurements, not passes.

WHAT COMES NEXT
Gate 3 must define and independently validate a physically meaningful
irreversible cutting law and reliable tool/fragment contact, retaining the
2 N stop and 0.1 mm penetration requirement. Recontact must not restore bonds.
Only then may Gate 4 build and qualify the actual tomato. No physical setup
has been adopted, no robot integration or training has started, and experimental
validation remains pending. The measured cost is a serious unresolved issue;
passing this phase must not be interpreted as a promise that Gates 3–6 will
produce an RL-speed model on this laptop.
