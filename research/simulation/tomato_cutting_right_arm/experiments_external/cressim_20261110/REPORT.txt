CRESSim-MPM bounded feasibility result — 10 November 2026

DECISION
The public CRESSim-MPM release builds and runs on this laptop, and its small
examples are fast. Do not adopt it unchanged as our arbitrary-cut tomato
environment. It failed the essential independent-fragment requirement after
knife withdrawal. No tomato, robot, kitchen, grip, trajectory or existing solver
file was edited. Nobody was contacted. No packages were installed.

MODEL DIFFERENCES — THESE ARE NOT TOMATO QUALIFICATION TESTS
Stock: approximately 1 m cube; 0.1 m particle spacing, 0.2 m grid, 1331 particles
at 1 kg each, gravity, 0.002 s integration step. Its cutter has 0.1 m fattening.
This unusually large/coarse example is useful for compatibility only.

Small block: 60 mm cube, 20 x 20 x 20 particles, 3 mm particle spacing, 6 mm
grid, 27 mg per particle, 216 g total, zero gravity, free outer surfaces.
No skin, gel, seeds, board, tomato geometry, measured cutting toughness or
damage/fracture law was added. The tool is the supplied analytical quad slicer,
80 x 80 mm with 0.5 mm fattening, not our CAD knife.
MLS: co-rotational elastic material, E = 100 kPa, nu = 0.4, dt = 0.0001 s.
PB: relaxation = 100, elasticity ratio = 0.9, 20 iterations, dt = 0.001 s.
PB's parameters are not Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio; they were not
calibrated to MLS. The speed comparison is not a material/accuracy comparison.
A 6 mm grid does not establish our project's 0.1 mm contact tolerance.

SEPARATION TEST AND CONTROLS
1. Sweep the slicer completely through x = 0, then clear the block.
2. Reorient outside the block and sweep through z = 0, then clear the block.
3. With the cutter clear, add 0.01 m/s tangential velocity to just one intended
   quarter. Compare against a separate otherwise identical unforced baseline.
   Labels only analyze the particles; they are not supplied to the solver.

Both sweeps completed with finite positions and velocities, but the intended
quarters were not mechanically independent. Untouched quarters reached up to
13.7% of the imposed pulse in MLS and 25.0% in PB. In PB, all four quarters
settled near one quarter of the originally imposed velocity within 0.1 s.
That is velocity sharing across the supposed cuts, not four independent pieces.

Independent control: initialize two halves with a nominal 1 mm gap. Give one
half a 0.01 m/s tangential pulse, with no gravity, friction or moving tool.
The screening threshold was an untouched-half velocity below 1% of the pulse.

Condition                    MLS peak transfer       PB peak transfer
Blade held in gap                    0%                      0%
Blade absent                       11.46%                   50.02%
Halves 24 mm apart                    0%                      0%

These reported controls are translated 1 mm relative to the grid so the blade
plane is not exactly on grid nodes. An earlier grid-aligned control also leaked
motion while the blade was held; all earlier data are preserved, not discarded.
This alignment sensitivity is another limitation. Translating the control did
not change engine code, material parameters or the predeclared 1% screen.

The close-gap test alone is not proof of a successful preceding fracture; it
isolates unwanted coupling between separate material groups. Together with the
two real sweeps and source inspection, it rules out claiming reliable persistent
slicing/dicing from this configuration. Wide separation can prevent sharing;
the result does not say that no visually separated example is possible.

SOURCE EXPLANATION
The slicer checks particle/grid-point sides using the CURRENT shape pose.
It temporarily changes transfers across that shape. There is no persistent
fracture identifier, cut history, bond failure or fracture-energy budget in
this tested path. Once the knife has left, the shared grid can couple nearby
particles again. This matches the measured controls.
Relevant pinned source locations:
src/solver/mpm_rigid_contact.h:402, checkOnDifferentSides()
src/solver/mls_mpm_algorithm.h:209, particle-to-grid filtering
src/solver/mls_mpm_algorithm.h:342, grid-to-particle filtering
src/solver/pb_mpm_algorithm.h:199 and 333, corresponding PB filtering
src/solver/constitutive_models.h:123, PB constraint parameters

FORCE AND STABILITY LIMITATIONS
The stock kinematic tool does not record reaction force, so its zero output
must not be interpreted as zero physical cutting resistance. In the small
tests, the dynamic flag enables impulse recording while the old kinematic
contact model and prescribed motion remain. The newer effective-mass contact
model was not used. Source inspection shows getShapeForce() contains accumulated
momentum exchange; the harness stores it and divides by actual integrated frame
duration for an unvalidated mean reaction estimate.

Baseline blade-pass reactions were essentially zero (maximum about 3.04e-7 N
MLS and 3.46e-6 N PB). In this coarse setup, the thin plane can pass between
particle layers and has no fracture toughness law to overcome. This is not a
validated tomato cutting-force curve and is not a physical cutting pass.
The blade path can change online without a premeshed seam, but that freedom
does not itself establish realistic fracture or contact forces.

All logged runs retained finite particle states and unchanged particle masses;
the block retained its 8000 particles. That only passes a basic numerical screen.
MLS also lost nearly all imposed rigid translation in the far-gap control,
despite no external load. A possible contributor is the hard-coded 1e-4 kg
grid-mass cutoff in mlsMpmUpdateGrid(), which skips conversion of low-mass nodes
from momentum to velocity. No causal patch or material qualification was
attempted. PB largely preserved translation in the far-gap control, but still
failed close-fragment independence and has uncalibrated material response.

RECOMMENDATION
Stop this adoption attempt at the failed separation gate. Do not integrate
CRESSim into the kitchen or start tomato RL on it. Its speed is promising,
but accepting coupled fragments would reward incorrect cutting behavior.
MPM remains a possible architecture; this public implementation would need
persistent fracture/separation and validated contact/material behavior before
it can replace the research model. That is additional method development,
not a configuration switch, and was deliberately not started in this study.
Keep the current project and its evidence unchanged while deciding that next
scope. The decisive acceptance test for any next candidate is two intersecting
cuts, full withdrawal, and independent fragment motion without hidden barriers.
