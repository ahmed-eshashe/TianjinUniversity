HEAD-VISION KNIFE PICKUP — separate v32 project
Requested 13 November 2026 (Asia/Shanghai)

Confirmed scope
- Right arm; actual finger contact and friction; no fixed attachment or suction.
- RGB-D sensing mounted at the robot's head/eye level, not an overhead camera.
- Center the board on the abdomen's forward midline, with board/counter support.
- Vary knife geometry and physically settled resting poses, positions and angles.
- Simulation first; later physical transfer. Existing cutting/DiSECt/v28-v31 files stay unchanged.
- Confirmed endpoint: secure pickup first; full cutting-grip adjustment is a separate later skill.

A. Scene and sensing
Create an isolated layer. Remove the mounted-tool joint and pickup clamp/cradle;
restore real hand/knife collisions, including reciprocal filtered-pair entries.
Move the board onto the abdomen midline without placing it over the sink or an
unsupported counter edge. Mount and calibrate one RGB-D sensor at eye level.
Check RGB, depth validity, board coverage, hand occlusion and frame conventions.
Keep raw actor inputs distinct from evaluator-only state and semantic labels.

B. Physical grasp feasibility
Audit existing handle geometry and contact approximations. Preserve hand joint
limits, provisional friction/effort parameters and previous evidence. First test
retention of an initialized supported hand pose; label this a component test,
not pickup. Then establish a free-body pickup from the board centre. The knife
must move only through gravity/contact after reset, with no kinematic drive or
attachment. Permit contact-based staged pickup, such as initial pinch then wrap;
label board-supported manoeuvres separately from unsupported holding. Stop on
blade contact, drops, excessive loads or invalid state. Do not train on false
successes or silently increase friction/force to obtain a pass.

C. Generalized task and evaluation
Use existing curved knife and tested straight-blade/handle as initial assets;
add documented geometric variants in separate assets with consistent masses and
colliders. Sample physically feasible board placements, settle them, and report
the reachable/visible region per knife. No universal any-knife/any-pose claim.
Hold out geometry families and placement/rotation bins before collecting data.
Count unsupported lift/hold, slip, blade contacts, collisions, drops and timeouts
independently of reward. Keep the stricter previous cutting-wrap rubric separate
if this new task initially accepts a secure pickup. Never relabel earlier failures.

D. Learning
Start with successful physical demonstrations or a state-informed training teacher,
then train a head-image + joint-feedback student. RGB/depth are actual rendered
sensor outputs. True object pose, segmentation IDs and contact labels are for
teacher/reward/evaluation only; they are not deployment actor inputs.
Use robot-relative wrist motion plus bounded finger commands with a low-level
joint controller; arm IK alone is not a learned pickup policy.
Randomize appearance, camera noise, placement and geometry in a curriculum.
Only expand to RL fine tuning after measured reset, throughput and success checks.

E. Evaluation and handover
Test fresh seeds and withheld knife geometry with the image actor alone; compare
against scripted/teacher baselines and report confidence intervals. Evaluate camera
occlusion and missing depth. Verify actual finger retention throughout lift/hold.
Show a live head-camera-driven pickup in the original kitchen, update the checklist,
and preserve checkpoints/configuration/data provenance. Simulation success does not
certify physical transfer or contact/calibration accuracy.
