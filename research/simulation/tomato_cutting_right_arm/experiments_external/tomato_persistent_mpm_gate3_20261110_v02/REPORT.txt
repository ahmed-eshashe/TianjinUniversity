GATE 3 — PHYSICAL QUALIFICATION FAILED; ISOLATED SETUP PRESERVED
10 November 2026

Outcome
The Gate 3 evaluation and bounded retest are complete, but Gate 3 has NOT passed.
No physical cut was created. The candidate is not adopted and Gate 4 must not start.
The report separates passing numerical components from the failed coupled model.

Primary blocker
The reconstructed material can deform without the corresponding constitutive
strain responding. In the first paid-cut pilot, stored determinant J remained
above 0.990 while the reconstructed geometry developed a negative corner J of
-0.458. This is local inversion, not legitimate fracture or a harmless rendering
issue. A particle-center contact check or stored-F volume check alone missed it.

An independent double-precision reference isolates a transfer null mode. The
512-particle / 343-node transfer has rank 343 and 169 particle-space null modes.
A nonrigid mode moves particles 0.25 micrometres in a 12.5-microsecond step while
stored F changes only 1.25e-17; the reconstructed geometry's F changes 2.56e-5.
This demonstrates a mechanically unobserved motion in the present formulation.
It is consistent with the loaded failure and cannot be removed by taking more
steps or just refining contact samples. It does not prove that one alternative
transfer will automatically solve every cutting issue.

Bounded correction and retest
The first test also exposed inadequate surface quadrature: at its saved final
state, the 250-micrometre samples report zero blade contact, while finer samples
and the volume certificate detect intrusion. A separate correction preserves
surface area but uses 62.5-micrometre sampling on the loaded and cut faces. A new
independent trilinear-volume Jacobian certificate retains the original J range
(0.1, 10). It rejects unproved states as well as resolved collapse.

The corrected pilot stopped at the last accepted time 0.029175 s (63.39 wall s).
The rejected next trial had reconstructed J = 0.09943. Peak force was 0.10558 N,
below 2 N. Accepted blade-penetration bounds stayed below 0.1 mm. Paid fracture
area and fracture work were both zero. It neither relaxed a stop nor opened an
unpaid interface. Positions, velocities, stored strain and codes were restored
to the last accepted state. A smaller-timestep positive-cut comparison is not
meaningful until a valid positive event exists.

Verified components and precise limits
1. Original blade CAD signed distance agrees with an independent CPU query to
   5.2e-8 m over the tested points; force, moment and power pullbacks pass.
   Conservative bounds cover the entire declared trilinear material geometry,
   rather than just particle centers. This reconstruction remains an approximation.
2. Shallow edge and broad-face contact load/unload numerical comparisons pass:
   maximum force differences 1.17% and 2.07%, positions below 0.24 micrometres,
   impulse/work differences below 0.22%, numerical energy residual below 1%.
   Force/support impulse balance is below 2e-13 N s. These are component passes,
   not physical cutting qualification; the geometry/strain discrepancy is visible
   even in these shallow tests and becomes destructive under stronger loading.
3. Area-based cutting-work accounting and transactional controls pass in isolation:
   area partition, half-step work, unpaid/duplicate rejection, query/retry safety,
   pause/reversal/re-entry and checkpoint reconstruction. Gc = 80 J/m² is synthetic,
   not a measured tomato property. This ideal work law differs from old cohesion.
4. Two pre-separated pieces collide, slide and separate without healing. Their
   history, pair force/moment and momentum checks pass. The original timestep
   pair fails instantaneous force and low-work refinement: 131% maximum force
   discrepancy under the frozen denominator-floor convention and a 1.43e-9 J
   friction-work discrepancy (14.3% under the 1e-8 J floor).
   One stiffness-derived 0.5-microsecond run completes. Its matched half-step run
   was not launched after the structural bulk/contact failure was established.
   Recontact refinement therefore remains unresolved, not passed.
5. The new volume-certificate controls pass independent identity, rotation,
   affine-compression, collapse and inversion tests, including rejection of the
   saved bad cutting state. The guard detects the defect; it does not repair it.

Still unqualified
Positive work-gated physical separation; integrated tip/spine negative controls;
blade contact on physically created new faces; matched refined material recontact;
and a temporally refined positive cut. A simple geometric opening, an accounting
unit test or a successful force-balance check is not substituted for these results.

Speed
All current full-contact tests use only 512 material particles on a 12 mm coupon.
Observed shallow CAD costs are roughly 900–2,160 wall s per simulated s, depending
on timestep and contact case. The corrected failed cutting pilot costs about
2,173 wall s/s. The very fine recontact run costs about 27,227 wall s/s. These
include contact and conservative geometry checks and evidence writing, without
rendering. They exclude initial reconstruction setup. They are not suitable for
interactive operation or RL. A complete 20 ms transition would cost tens to
hundreds of wall seconds at these rates. Do not extrapolate equivalent tomato
performance from these coupon tests, or use the old bulk-only speed as full-step
speed. Speed optimization is premature while the coupled physics is invalid.
