02Make simulation practical for learningIn parallel0 / 5⌄

Faster runs must still meet the approved accuracy checks.

  • 2.1Measure where simulation time is spent.
  • 2.2Test faster settings against the approved accuracy limits.
  • 2.3Check accuracy over a complete cut—not only short windows.
  • 2.4Measure the resulting time per training episode.
  • 2.5Decide whether repeated simulation training is feasible.
03Build the learning interfaceCan prepare now0 / 6⌄

Give the policy a small, clearly bounded job.

  • 3.1 · IN PROGRESSDefine observations: relative knife pose, velocity, force, and task phase.
  • 3.2Define actions: small changes to downward speed and sideways motion.
  • 3.3Specify control frequency, units, scaling, and action limits.
  • 3.4Keep collision stops and force limits outside the learned policy.
  • 3.5 · IN PROGRESSKeep hidden damage information separate from policy inputs.
  • 3.6Verify that zero learned adjustment reproduces the scripted baseline.
04Build repeatable episodesCan prepare now0 / 5⌄

Every trial needs a clean start and an honest outcome.

  • 4.1Reset robot, knife, tomato deformation, and damage correctly.
  • 4.2Verify repeated resets produce consistent starting states.
  • 4.3Detect full separation, withdrawal, and supported-slice success.
  • 4.4Detect collision, excessive force, numerical failure, and timeout.
  • 4.5 · IN PROGRESSRecord observations, commands, outcomes, and configuration for every trial.
05Collect demonstration dataAfter baseline0 / 4⌄

Successful and unsuccessful trials must stay distinguishable.

  • 5.1Record successful scripted cuts.
  • 5.2Label partial cuts and failures accurately.
  • 5.3Collect controlled variations in tomato position and material settings.
  • 5.4Split complete trials into training, validation, and held-out test sets.
06Train through imitationAfter demonstrations0 / 4⌄

Learn from saved decisions before trying repeated simulation trials.

  • 6.1Train an initial policy on recorded demonstrations.
  • 6.2Check its predictions on held-out recordings.
  • 6.3Test it in simulation with action limits enforced.
  • 6.4Compare its cutting success and forces with the scripted baseline.
07Learn small correctionsAfter imitation + speed gate0 / 5⌄

Scripted motion plus a bounded learned adjustment.

  • 7.1Combine scripted motion with bounded policy adjustments.
  • 7.2Define rewards that favor verified separation and controlled forces.
  • 7.3Check that rewards cannot be satisfied by pushing the tomato away or exploiting simulation errors.
  • 7.4Train only after simulation speed and reset reliability are sufficient.
  • 7.5Preserve a reproducible baseline for comparison.
08Evaluate and reportFinal evaluation0 / 5⌄

Show whether learning improves the result on unseen cases.

  • 8.1Test unseen tomato positions and material settings.
  • 8.2Compare scripted, imitation, and corrected-policy performance.
  • 8.3Report success rate, peak force, collisions, and completion time.
  • 8.4Save reproducible configurations and representative failures.
  • 8.5Keep experimental validation and motor-work qualification pending until independently resolved.
